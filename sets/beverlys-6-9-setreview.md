The structure largely holds — but two things don't fully land as described.

The oscillation you're describing architecturally is valid as a concept, but in execution it's more of a "settle in" section that gets disrupted once, rather than a back-and-forth.

**The middle section (18–29) is the most complex and least legible as a macro phase.** Six sad/mad tracks followed by Uncle Cracker (personal), a tone-set reset (Rip Able), another sad/mad (The Dream), then a personal+bread/butter alternation (Metallica→Bizzy→Holy Fam→Lil Bump). This is a good *micro* structure — it breathes — but it doesn't map cleanly onto "personal touch" followed by "bread/butter but more personal" as two distinct beats. They're interwoven rather than sequential.

## Macro Flow — Overall Assessment

**What works:**

The doomer block is the strongest structural element. Four tracks in a row, all comf, escalating from accessible (Steklovata pop) to demanding (Jesu drone) within the category. This is how to handle the high-risk material — cluster it, let it breathe, build depth within the block.

The Staind closer coming after doomer is the right emotional architecture. Ending on recognizable human grief rather than alien dissolution means the crowd leaves with something to hold.

The bread/butter section (13–17) is clean and correctly placed — it's the most legible section of the set and serves as a landing zone between the opening personal material and the emotional weight to come.

The internal pacing of the sad/mad cluster (18–23) is good: three Future-adjacent tracks (comf), then a mixed disruption (Sad Bitch Song), then an ints peak (Springsteen), then a release valve (Uncle Cracker). That's a small arc within the arc.

**What to think about:**

The "disrupt/oscillation" phase (5–12) reads as a long comfort section with a single disruption rather than true oscillation. If oscillation is the intent, the Thunder (ints) track at position 11 needs company earlier — something ints in the 5–8 range that then resolves back to comf before Thunder arrives. As currently ordered, it's a comfort plateau with a late spike.

The middle section (18–29) is doing several things at once — sad/mad, personal, B&B, another tone-set reset — and the seams show slightly. Not a problem for the crowd (they won't be tracking categories), but against the macro you described, it's one big heterogeneous block rather than two distinct phases (personal → B&B more personal).

The two Project Pat versions at 14–15 back-to-back will be the most obviously structural moment in the set — a demonstration rather than a flow. Whether that's welcome depends on what the room needs at that point.

## One Thing the Categorization System Reveals

Your use of **comf** for tracks like DMX "Slippin'", Future, and even the doomer block is the most sophisticated aspect of the categorization. You're distinguishing between *emotional intensity* (which can be high while comf) and *physical activation* (which comf specifically excludes). This is a real and important distinction that most people don't name explicitly. The doomer tracks are all comf — they don't make you move, they make you still. DMX is comf for the same reason. That's not the conventional use of "comfortable" but it's the correct one for your purposes.

The system would benefit from having that distinction made explicit somewhere — because "comf" on Jesu and "comf" on R Kelly are describing two completely different listener states that happen to share the quality of not-activating.
